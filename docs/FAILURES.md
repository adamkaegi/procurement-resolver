# Resolution Failure Catalogue

These are deliberately preserved cases for resolution review. They describe
failure modes and root causes, not measured production metrics.

1. Numbered companies with identical legal names can be the same entity only when jurisdiction and context agree; name-only matching cannot distinguish them.
2. Numbered companies with different provincial suffixes are distinct legal names; numeric-prefix similarity is not sufficient evidence.
3. An operating name such as `Davie Canada` may omit the legal prefix `Chantier`; the relationship needs corroborating context.
4. A legal suffix omission such as `IBM Canada Limited` versus `IBM Canada Ltd.` is a normalization case, not a new entity.
5. A parent such as `Davie Holdings Ltd.` must not be merged with `Chantier Davie Canada Inc.`; the root cause is corporate hierarchy, which this resolver does not model.
6. A subsidiary such as `Microsoft Canada Inc.` must not be merged with `Microsoft Corporation`; the root cause is parent/subsidiary ambiguity.
7. Divisions recorded as `X Canada` and `X Public Sector` can share a parent but are not necessarily the same contracting entity.
8. Punctuation and casing corruption can be removed deterministically, but an altered token such as `Data Services` versus `Data Systems` requires abstention or adjudication.
9. A commodity or opportunity title can look like a vendor name; the root cause is source semantics, not fuzzy matching quality.
10. A government buyer name can appear in a vendor field after data-entry error; buyer context is required to avoid a false match.
11. Ontario-incorporated firms absent from the federal registry cannot be rejected merely because no registry candidate exists.
12. Empty, footer, and note rows are not vendors; source-row filtering must happen before normalization.
13. Ottawa Transit reports overlap the broader delegation-of-authority period reports; the same contract ID can occur in multiple PDFs and requires deterministic deduplication.
14. ~~One Ottawa PDF is image-only and produces no text through the deterministic parser; OCR or a model-assisted extraction path is required for that document.~~ **Retracted — this was a misdiagnosis.** Re-verified against all eight archived PDFs: none contains a single embedded image, every one yields substantial extractable text, and every one contributes records to the deduped warehouse (the file previously called image-only yields 32,823 characters of text and 98 records, 50 of which survive cross-report deduplication). The original entry almost certainly described a parser failure on that report's layout — later fixed by other parser work — rather than a scanned document. Kept here rather than deleted because a retracted failure claim is itself a finding: "the parser produced nothing" and "the document contains nothing" are different diagnoses, and the catalogue asserted the harder one without verifying it.
15. Ottawa report tables place the currency symbol before or after the amount depending on the report period; amount parsing must support both layouts.
16. Some Ottawa records have no non-competitive rationale or description; those fields must remain nullable rather than being inferred.
17. **Fixed.** `token_set_ratio` scored a name whose tokens are a strict superset of another's as a full match regardless of meaning: `classify_pair("Acme Consulting Inc.", "Acme Consulting Group Inc.")` returned 100.0 and auto-accepted, though `evals/provisional/resolution/pairs.csv` (P015) labels it `no-match`. The root cause was the scoring function comparing the shared-token intersection rather than the full names. At warehouse scale this was not a corner case: it auto-accepted 3,958 entity pairs, including a 30-character firm name matched at 100.0 against a 620-character multi-vendor roster. Switched to `token_sort_ratio`, which compares full sorted strings so extra tokens cost score: auto-accepts dropped to 987, the Acme pair now abstains at 83.3, and `"Bell"` vs `"Bell Canada"` now scores 53.3 and is rejected instead of matching at 100.0. Regression-pinned by `tests/test_resolve.py::test_token_superset_is_rejected_not_matched`. The trade this bought is catalogued as #18.

18. Changing the scorer to fix #17 traded one failure profile for another, and the new one is narrower but real. Against the 30-pair provisional file, `token_sort_ratio` now misses `CGI Information Systems and Management Consultants Inc.` vs `CGI Inc.` (scores 11.3 — a genuine legal-name shortening, labelled `match`), because the scorer penalises extreme length asymmetry that `token_set_ratio` ignored by construction. It also still accepts `Canada Clean Supplies` vs `Canada Cleaning Supplies` at 93.3 (labelled `no-match`): a single-word morphological variant is not something string similarity can separate from a real variant spelling. Net effect on the provisional numbers: federal↔on precision 0.727 → 0.875, federal↔federal recall 1.0 → 0.667. Precision improved, recall on drastic abbreviations regressed. Closing the abbreviation gap needs corroborating evidence (shared buyer, shared contract identifiers, or a registry anchor), not a different string metric.

19. Two provisional labels are themselves contestable, which limits what the numbers above can prove. `Northstar Engineering Ltd.` vs `North Star Engineering Limited` is labelled `no-match` ("token variation requires evidence") but reads as the same firm to a human and now scores 97.7; `Chantier Davie Canada Inc.` vs `Davie Canada Inc.` is labelled `match` and correctly abstains at 72.7 given #3's reasoning that the relationship needs corroboration. The provisional pairs are model-generated scaffolding, not a hand-labelled set, so these were left as-is rather than relabelled to flatter the scorer — but any precision/recall computed from them inherits their label quality, which is exactly why no figure from that file is reported as a project metric.

20. A table row spanning a PDF page break made the Ottawa parser absorb the next page's header, footer, and column labels into the vendor name — 97 corrupted names, worst case 620 characters (`"DILLON CONSULTING LIMITED CONTRACTS > $25,000 AWARDED UNDER THE DELEGATION OF AUTHORITY FOR THE PERIOD OF JANUARY 1, 2024 TO JUNE 30, 2024 Page 1 of 28..."`). Root cause: vendor was defined as "all text after the amount to the end of the flattened block", with no page-boundary guard. Invisible for the entire life of the project because exact-normalized clustering never compared one entity against another; it surfaced immediately once fuzzy blocking did, as pairs of unrelated vendors scoring >92 on their shared boilerplate. Fixed by cutting known page furniture off the vendor field (97 → 0).

21. Some Ottawa delegated-authority rows award one contract to a roster of firms, so the vendor field legitimately holds many companies (observed: 60 firms, 620 characters, comma-separated). These are flagged `_provenance.multi_vendor_row` and excluded from entity resolution rather than split on punctuation — splitting would invent vendors from names that legitimately contain commas (`"ARCADIS PROF SERVICES (CANADA) INC"`). The release stays in the warehouse with its verbatim name; it simply asserts nothing about any single vendor.
